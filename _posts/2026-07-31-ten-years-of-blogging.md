<p>It has been 10 years since my <a
href="https://lgmoneda.github.io/2016/04/02/hello-world.html">Hello
World</a> post on this blog. I recall the feeling of "oh, only if I had
a blog set up, I'd write frequently!". It took me <strong>a
year</strong> to write the first post after the initial test post. This
event introduced a new practice for me: whenever there isn't a high cost
to setting up something that would enable my "amazing drive to do a
certain activity", I'd do it to put my will and skills to the test.</p>
<p>Writing has been a pleasure since then. Self-prompted writing and
building is a joy that I regret discovering so late in my life. Writing,
reflecting, thinking, building, understanding, and many other
meaning-making activities are closely intertwined, so regardless of
whether I started with a desire to write or arrived at it eventually
through the other activities, I'd have a good time. I also can't
disentangle writing from the tool I've been using. Emacs has been such a
great companion over the years, enabling me to do everything I do
related to thinking in a single digital tool: blogging, studying,
researching, writing papers, writing my MSc thesis, coding, etc.</p>
<p>I'm quite happy that the effort I put into building and understanding
hard concepts was useful to people all around the world. Posts like <a
href="https://lgmoneda.github.io/2017/03/15/elisp-summary.html">elisp
introduction</a>, <a
href="https://lgmoneda.github.io/2021/05/27/invariant-risk-minimization.html">invariance
in machine learning</a>, <a
href="https://lgmoneda.github.io/2021/02/19/causal-invariance.html">independent
causal mechanism</a>, and <a
href="https://lgmoneda.github.io/2021/01/12/spurious-correlation-ml-and-causality.html">spurious
correlation</a> sum around ten thousands of views. There is even a
citation in a scientific paper:
<a id="cite-ge22_invar_struc_learn_better_gener_causal_explain-1"></a><span
class="citation"
data-cites="ge22_invar_struc_learn_better_gener_causal_explain">Ge
<em>et al.</em> (<a
href="#ref-ge22_invar_struc_learn_better_gener_causal_explain"
role="doc-biblioref">2022</a>)</span>.</p>
<p>This created a second practice on me: Planning to take
longer. Why would I bother writing something others can easily find
in other places on the internet or realize by themselves quickly? I
realized I can contribute further by dedicating time and energy to a
topic, creating clear explanations, figures, and code examples, and then
sharing them with the community. This takes time. So I started managing
my " finishing anxiety " better better, and enjoy taking a long time to
craft.</p>
<p>My post about "<a
href="https://lgmoneda.github.io/2020/01/12/one-on-one-guide.html">one-on-ones</a>",
which I wrote to help myself, became the most-visited post on my blog.
In addition to my desire to publish about project management for Data
Science, a topic that was key in my career growth, it motivated me to
spin up the <a href="https://datascienceleadership.com/">Data Science
Leadership</a> web-book, which was super useful to communicate with my
coworkers. I eventually get a message or meet people who mention it to
me. By the way, people coming from nowhere on LinkedIn to say thanks for
the content I share here is a super great feeling.</p>
<p>Curiously, my principle of taking longer and making things worth
sharing also pushed me to create a hidden subsection on my blog where I
can write short-form text and publish it. I was also drawn to <a
href="https://www.casafernandopessoa.pt/en/visit/temporary-exhibitions/game-disquiet/Construction/Loose-papers-found-inside-the-trunk?utm_source=chatgpt.com">Fernando
Pessoa's trunk</a>, where he stored more than 25k pages from a myriad of
written projects, and from where later people could organize the "Book
of Disquiet", <a id="cite-pessoa2006livro-1"></a><span class="citation"
data-cites="pessoa2006livro">(<a href="#ref-pessoa2006livro"
role="doc-biblioref">Pessoa, 2006</a>)</span>, which is in my current
top 3 favorite books. In my own trunk, which I call "the attic", I can
mix whatever topics I want; I don't mind mixing posts in different
languages, publishing a single image, or sharing pictures, lyrics, or a
joke. Again, I did it to expose my will and creativity to a frictionless
creation experience and to stop making excuses, which forced me to look
at my real gaps in creative processes. I don't see how I would get to
this point without the blogging journey.</p>
<p>A colleague used to joke that when someone creates a blog or gets
back to blogging, they are trying to get a new job. I gladly realized I
didn't create for this reason, nor did I stop blogging after I got my
last job, which I've been in for 9 years now. Though it certainly
reflects my career, I'm proud of this little space of mine on the
internet.</p>
