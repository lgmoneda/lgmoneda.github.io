À la recherche du temps perdu

Essa é uma jornada ilustrada pela obra monumental de Marcel Proust, "Em Busca do Tempo Perdido". Esta experiência imersiva combina as profundas reflexões do autor sobre memória, tempo e natureza humana com recursos visuais para dar vida às suas paisagens literárias.

E edição utilizada para referência é a da Globo.

Ao percorrer estas referências ilustradas, você encontrará os principais locais, temas e momentos que definem esta obra-prima — desde a infância do narrador em Combray até as revelações do tempo recuperado. Cada seção oferece trechos textuais e interpretações visuais que iluminam a extraordinária visão literária de Proust.

Combray

For a long time I used to go to bed early. Sometimes, when I had put out my candle, my eyes would close so quickly that I had not even time to say "I'm going to sleep." And half an hour later the thought that it was time to go to sleep would awaken me.

The real voyage of discovery consists not in seeking new landscapes, but in having new eyes. In Combray, every street corner held a memory, every garden gate whispered secrets of the past. The hawthorn blossoms along the Méséglise way carried with them the essence of childhood summers.

Memory is a strange thing. It can transport us across decades in an instant, triggered by the simplest sensation—the taste of a madeleine dipped in tea, the scent of lime blossoms on a summer evening.

The Méséglise Way

The two ways from Combray, Méséglise and Guermantes, have remained for me the prototype of all the ways that lead through life. They seemed to me to be as different from each other as the East is from the West.

Along the Méséglise way, we would walk past Swann's property, catching glimpses through the hedge of his garden where pink hawthorns bloomed with an almost painful beauty. The very air seemed different here, charged with mystery.

I remember how the light fell differently on the Méséglise way, how it seemed to illuminate not just the landscape but something deeper—the very essence of longing itself. The path wound through meadows where poppies blazed like scattered rubies.

Swann's Way

Swann was one of those men who, having lived for a long time amid the illusions of love, have seen the most diverse types of women enhance or impair the happiness, the disappointments, the sufferings that they have caused him.

The peculiar smile with which she would greet him sometimes in the early days of their acquaintance, when he was not yet in love with her, came back to him now with a mystery that it had never had for him then.

And so it was that Swann's love for Odette became the great passion of his life, transforming him from a man of refined tastes and social ease into someone capable of the deepest suffering and the most exquisite joy.

Time Regained

The real paradises are the paradises that we have lost. In the garden at night, under the starry sky, I understood that time was not lost but transformed, crystallized into art, into memory, into the eternal present of consciousness.

All our final decisions are made in a state of mind that is not going to last. Yet somehow, in the quiet contemplation of memory, we find that what seemed lost forever has been preserved in the amber of recollection.

The true journey of discovery consists not in seeking new landscapes, but in having new eyes. And in the end, we discover that the time we thought was lost was never lost at all—it was simply waiting for us to find the key that would unlock its treasures.
